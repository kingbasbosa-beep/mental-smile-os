# ENGLISH LANGUAGE CONSTITUTION

## Purpose

This constitution governs English generated for Mental Smile. English must
express registered constitutional meaning without becoming the source from
which Arabic is mechanically translated.

## Voice

English must be:

- plain and calm;
- respectful and person-led;
- specific about actions and states;
- supportive without claiming ownership;
- concise for interfaces;
- honest about uncertainty and system boundaries.

## Consistency

- Use one registered term for one governed concept.
- Do not alternate between provider, clinician, specialist, or professional
  unless their term cards define distinct meanings.
- Use sentence case for interface text unless a product convention requires
  otherwise.
- Use the same state vocabulary across cards, messages, and observatories.
- Do not shorten a constitutional concept into an ambiguous operational label.

## Person-First Language

- Refer to a person, their preferences, or their chosen identity.
- Do not reduce a person to a case, condition, signal, account, or request.
- Avoid permanent labels for temporary needs or circumstances.
- Respect self-description without inferring identity.

Preferred:

- `People with accessibility preferences`
- `Support you choose`
- `Signals you chose to share`
- `Explore available resources`

Avoid:

- `Disabled user`
- `The case`
- `The addict`
- `Assigned patient`
- `Non-compliant client`

## Accessibility-First Language

- Describe available formats, choices, and control behavior.
- Treat access as a property of the system.
- Prefer `accessibility preferences`, `communication preferences`, and
  `accessible format`.
- Avoid `special needs` as a general identity category.
- Never imply that accessibility support depends on approval.

## Recovery-First Language

- Recovery is person-led, non-linear, and not owned by the platform.
- Use hope, growth, continuity, strengths, and chosen support.
- Describe opportunities rather than guaranteed outcomes.
- Do not use compliance language to measure a person.

Preferred:

- `Resources that may support your direction`
- `Choose what feels useful to you`
- `Continue when you are ready`

Avoid:

- `You must follow the plan`
- `We manage your recovery`
- `The right pathway has been assigned to you`

## Non-Diagnostic Language

- Signals are not diagnoses.
- Do not infer a condition, severity, treatment, or clinical need.
- Use explicit uncertainty when the evidence is incomplete.
- Avoid `You are...` and `You need...` conclusions derived from behavior.
- A recommendation must remain a possibility: `may be useful`, `you can
  explore`, or `based on signals you chose to share`.

## Agency

Preferred action verbs:

- Explore
- Choose
- View
- Share
- Contact
- Save
- Continue
- Learn

Blocked authority verbs in active person-facing doctrine:

- Approve
- Reject
- Assign
- Activate
- Control
- Manage case
- Own journey

Direct imperatives are acceptable for obvious controls and verified safety
instructions, not for controlling personal decisions.

## Readiness and Visibility

- Use `Ready`, `Incomplete`, `Draft`, and `Blocked` only when they describe the
  governed state accurately.
- Readiness is derived from signal completeness.
- Visibility follows readiness and independent safety boundaries.
- Do not use `Approved`, `Rejected`, `Pending approval`, or `Activated` as
  visibility language.

## Monitoring

- Monitoring observes declared state, safety signals, freshness, or readiness.
- It does not approve, route, allocate, or own work.
- Prefer `Observatory`, `Signals`, `Readiness`, and `Visibility`.
- Avoid `Control Room`, `Operations Queue`, and `Admin Dashboard`.

## Forbidden Wording

Block wording that expresses:

- booking ownership;
- payment or payout approval;
- assignment to a clinician or support owner;
- case ownership;
- session control;
- administrative activation;
- review queues with decision authority;
- platform ownership of recovery;
- inferred diagnosis;
- guaranteed outcomes;
- deficit-first identity.

Synonyms are also blocked when they preserve the same doctrine.

## Preferred English Patterns

| Purpose | Preferred Pattern |
|---|---|
| Navigation | `Open {destination}` |
| Discovery | `Explore {resources}` |
| Optional support | `Contact technical support` |
| Signal sharing | `Share the signals you choose` |
| Readiness | `Readiness: {state}` |
| Missing information | `Add {item} to improve readiness` |
| Recommendation | `{resource} may be relevant to your signals` |
| Empty state | `No {items} yet` |
| Recoverable error | `We couldn't {action}. Try again.` |
| Safety | `If there is immediate danger, contact local emergency services.` |

## English Quality Checks

Before acceptance, verify:

1. registered vocabulary is used consistently;
2. the copy names the action or state precisely;
3. agency is preserved;
4. no diagnosis, stigma, or unsupported promise appears;
5. authority boundaries remain visible;
6. tone is proportional to context;
7. Arabic carries equivalent meaning, urgency, and uncertainty;
8. the output follows its surface pattern.

## English Parity Rule

English must not become more promotional, clinical, certain, or institutionally
powerful than Arabic. Neither language is allowed to silently change doctrine.
