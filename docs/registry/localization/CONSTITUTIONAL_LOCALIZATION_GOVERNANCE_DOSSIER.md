# CONSTITUTIONAL LOCALIZATION GOVERNANCE DOSSIER

Role: Mental Smile Constitutional Governance Auditor  
Purpose: Transform localization from a translation store into a governed federation language system  
Mode: Strategic governance only

## Executive Context

Mental Smile localization currently carries language from four eras:

1. Legacy Booking Era
2. Operational Platform Era
3. Marketplace Transition Era
4. Constitutional Federation Era

The present system contains 205 localization keys, 95 directly referenced keys, 106 keys without direct non-generated consumers, one locale parity gap, 22 duplicate-meaning groups, and 771 old-doctrine key or hardcoded candidates.

The hardcoded inventory contains 7003 machine-detected candidates across current and archived Dart. That number is an intake population, not a validated count of user-facing sentences: the discovery scanner also captured code fragments and multiline literals. Governance must therefore qualify each candidate before it becomes a constitutional language record.

This dossier governs authority, identity, and system meaning. It does not translate, rename, delete, or migrate strings.

## Phase 1: Language Constitution

### Constitutional Principle

Language does not merely describe the system. It declares:

- Who has agency.
- Who owns a choice.
- Whether a relationship is voluntary.
- Whether the system observes or commands.
- Whether readiness is derived or granted.
- Whether support is available or imposed.

Constitutional language must preserve human choice, distributed ownership, signal-based readiness, observational monitoring, and clear safety boundaries.

### Constitutional Vocabulary

| Term | Constitutional Meaning | Why It Belongs |
|---|---|---|
| Provider | A discoverable person or organization offering a capability | Supports marketplace plurality without platform ownership |
| Center | A declared service destination with capabilities, resources, and visibility readiness | Preserves entity identity without operational control |
| Service | A defined offering that a person may discover or choose | Describes value without creating a managed lifecycle |
| Resource | Content, contact, destination, tool, or external aid | Supports choice and continuity without ownership |
| Support | Assistance offered without possession of a person or journey | Compatible with voluntary human choice |
| Recovery | A person-led direction of hope, continuity, and growth | Avoids platform ownership of outcomes |
| Accessibility | Preferences and conditions that make participation possible | Establishes inclusion as a first-class system concern |
| Knowledge | Structured guidance, education, policy, and learning material | Supports informed choice |
| Partnership | A voluntary relationship between independent participants | Replaces centralized platform control |
| District | A constitutional navigation area containing related rooms and registries | Organizes the federation without implying hierarchy |
| Registry | A governed source of definitions, identities, resources, or signals | Establishes traceability and single-source meaning |
| Signal | An observation or expression that may attract resources | Does not diagnose or grant authority |
| Capability | A declared ability, service, accessibility feature, or resource | Enables discovery without approval doctrine |
| Tenant | A bounded participant or organizational context with its own data scope | Supports federated separation when technically applicable |
| Governance | Rules that constrain the system and make decisions auditable | Replaces discretionary administrative ownership |
| Monitoring | Read-only observation of signals, readiness, safety, and system health | Observes without owning outcomes |
| Federation | Independent rooms, registries, and participants connected by declared contracts | Defines the target architecture |
| Owner Room | Constitutional stewardship and system-boundary visibility | Owner is a steward, not an admin operator |
| Monitoring Room | Observation of readiness, signals, and safety conditions | Makes observability explicit and non-operational |
| Readiness | A derived state based on signal completeness | Replaces human activation or approval |
| Declaration | Information submitted by its source for visibility and discovery | Replaces approval-shaped request language |
| Visibility | Eligibility to appear in discovery based on readiness and safety | Separates discovery from authority |
| Recommendation | A signal-informed option presented without assignment | Preserves human choice |
| Observatory | A read-only surface for governed observation | Replaces operational workspace and queue language |
| Personal Space | A person-controlled environment for identity, preferences, and signals | Prevents platform ownership of the client journey |

### Transitional Vocabulary

Transitional terms are not equally acceptable. Each receives one disposition.

| Term | Disposition | Governance Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Request | RENAME LATER | Allowed only for voluntary contact or technical help; replace approval-shaped requests with declaration, contact, or support signal |
| Review | RENAME LATER | Allowed for document examination or self-review; replace authority review with readiness observation |
| Approval | FREEZE | No new keys or runtime copy; existing occurrences are compatibility evidence only |
| Pending | RENAME LATER | Replace vague pending states with incomplete, submitted, processing, awaiting information, or unavailable |
| Assignment | FREEZE | No new use outside historical documentation; recommendations must not become assignments |
| Status | KEEP WITH SCOPE | Valid only when the state owner and derivation are explicit, such as readiness status or safety status |
| Case | RENAME LATER | Replace with conversation, support signal, safety report, declaration, or contact according to meaning |
| Inbox | RENAME LATER | Replace operational inboxes with messages, conversations, declarations, or observed signals |
| Dashboard | RENAME LATER | Replace room-specific dashboards with Room, Observatory, Registry, or Personal Space where appropriate |
| Active | FREEZE AS GENERIC AUTHORITY | May describe a connection or current selection, but not human-granted account activation |
| Completed | KEEP WITH SCOPE | Valid for a user action or submitted process; must not imply ownership of recovery |
| Follow-up | RENAME LATER | Allowed only as person-chosen continuity support; forbidden as organizational ownership lifecycle |

### Forbidden Vocabulary

| Forbidden Term | Conflict With Federation Governance |
|---|---|
| Booking | Encodes an extinct transaction and lifecycle model |
| Booking Owner | Assigns ownership of a person's service journey |
| Payout | Reintroduces the extinct platform financial lifecycle |
| Accounting Review | Creates centralized financial authority outside the constitutional system |
| Payment Approval | Grants humans authority over financial progression |
| Queue Owner | Converts observation into operational possession |
| Session Control | Implies the platform controls human interaction |
| Admin Ownership | Restores centralized authority already abolished |
| Admin Control Room | Recreates an extinct command hierarchy |
| Lifecycle Ownership | Allows the system to possess another participant's journey |
| Case Owner | Turns a person or conversation into assigned operational property |
| Manual Assignment | Replaces recommendation and choice with centralized routing |
| Approve Provider | Contradicts readiness-derived visibility |
| Reject Center | Reintroduces human visibility authority |
| Activation Authority | Contradicts signal completeness and safety separation |
| Operational Queue | Implies centralized work ownership and decision rights |

Forbidden terms may appear only in explicit historical records under LEGACY_IMPORTS. They cannot be introduced into active keys, cards, labels, routes, reports, or generated copy.

## Phase 3: Legacy Doctrine Detection

### Booking Era Remnants

Evidence includes:

- `webClinicianSessionPrice`
- `webClinicianSessionDuration`
- `webCenterMissingSession`
- residency scheduling and rescheduling status keys
- hardcoded references to home-visit sessions
- archived booking and session vocabulary

Constitutional finding: these terms preserve a managed service lifecycle. They must remain frozen until each consumer is classified as historical, marketplace information, or a new resource/capability concept.

### Operational Control Remnants

Evidence includes:

- `centerInboxTitle`
- `clinicianInboxTitle`
- `clinicianOpenCases`
- `clinicianAcceptRequest`
- `clinicianRejectRequest`
- `clinicianActiveRequests`
- `statusRequestCoordination`
- `statusRequestUpdateInProgress`

Constitutional finding: these terms describe work queues, decisions, and ownership. They belong to the Operational Platform Era and conflict with recommendation, declaration, and observational governance.

### Admin-Centric Remnants

Evidence includes:

- `centerRequestViaAdmin` as a key name, even though its displayed value now says contact the center.
- registration wording that still describes accounts as being reviewed.
- hardcoded admin and review terminology in current and archived sources.

Constitutional finding: changing the display value without changing the governed key meaning leaves an administrative fossil in the language system.

### Approval Doctrine Remnants

Evidence includes:

- submit-for-review wording.
- browse-library-during-review wording.
- approval, rejection, accept, and pending terminology.
- rejection-reason and confirm-rejection keys.

Constitutional finding: the wording still models humans granting progression. The target model is declaration, completeness, readiness, visibility, and safety separation.

### Platform-Centric Remnants

Evidence includes:

- open cases.
- referred chat cases.
- request coordination.
- center and clinician inboxes.
- follow-up and lifecycle status language.

Constitutional finding: the platform is still linguistically presented as a coordinator and owner. Federation language must present options, resources, signals, and human choice.

## Phase 4: Language Sovereignty Model

### Who Owns Vocabulary?

Vocabulary is owned by constitutional registries, not by individual pages, developers, translators, or generated files.

Each term has:

- One vocabulary owner.
- Approved meanings.
- Allowed contexts.
- Prohibited meanings.
- Locale-specific expressions.
- Lifecycle status.

### Who Can Introduce New Terminology?

A feature or registry steward may propose terminology. Introduction requires:

1. Owning registry.
2. Defined concept and user impact.
3. English and Arabic semantic pair.
4. Doctrine classification.
5. Consumer list.
6. Conflict search against approved and deprecated vocabulary.
7. Accessibility and dignity review.

No generated tool, AI assistant, or page author may create constitutional vocabulary independently.

### How Terminology Becomes Approved

```text
PROPOSED
-> REGISTRY_OWNED
-> SEMANTICALLY_REVIEWED
-> LOCALE_ALIGNED
-> DOCTRINE_COMPLIANT
-> APPROVED
```

"Approved" here describes governance of terminology, not authority over people, providers, or visibility.

### How Terminology Becomes Deprecated

A term is deprecated when:

- Its underlying doctrine is extinct.
- A clearer constitutional term exists.
- It creates ambiguity about authority or ownership.
- It conflicts with dignity, accessibility, or human choice.
- Its consumers have moved to another concept.

Deprecation records the replacement term, affected keys, consumers, and sunset phase.

### How Future Migrations Occur

Terminology migrations proceed registry by registry:

1. Freeze new use of the deprecated term.
2. Inventory keys and hardcoded consumers.
3. Define replacement meaning, not merely replacement wording.
4. Create new governed keys.
5. Migrate active consumers.
6. Retain aliases only if runtime compatibility is proven.
7. Regenerate localization outputs.
8. Verify both locales and accessibility.
9. Archive the old term as historical doctrine.

## Phase 5: Localization Registry Architecture

```text
Language Constitution
        |
Vocabulary Registry Layer
        |
Terminology Governance Layer
        |
Localization Registry Layer
        |
Doctrine Compliance Layer
        |
Language Audit Layer
        |
Future AI Language Validation Layer
```

### Vocabulary Registry Layer

Defines concepts, owners, approved terms, deprecated terms, forbidden meanings, and future vocabulary.

### Terminology Governance Layer

Controls proposal, review, approval, deprecation, replacement, and doctrine versioning.

### Localization Registry Layer

Maps governed concepts to locale keys, Arabic text, English text, placeholders, consumers, and generated outputs.

### Doctrine Compliance Layer

Rejects new vocabulary that restores approval, assignment, booking, admin ownership, diagnosis, stigma, or platform possession.

### Language Audit Layer

Observes:

- Key parity.
- Consumer references.
- hardcoded candidates.
- duplicate meanings.
- deprecated terms.
- forbidden terms.
- mixed-language drift.
- locale drift.

### Future AI Language Validation Layer

AI may:

- detect terminology drift.
- propose registry ownership.
- compare meanings across locales.
- identify authority implications.
- detect stigma and deficit language.
- suggest compliant alternatives.

AI may not:

- approve its own vocabulary.
- bypass registry ownership.
- silently rewrite constitutional terms.
- infer authority from historical language.

## Phase 6: Hardcoded String Strategy

### Governance Finding

The 7003-item discovery inventory is not a clean migration queue. It includes:

- probable user-facing strings.
- model labels.
- developer messages.
- route and field fragments.
- archived source strings.
- multiline code fragments captured by the scanner.

Therefore, the first governance action is qualification, not migration.

### Classification Framework

| Classification | Meaning | Governance Destination |
|---|---|---|
| Constitutional | Current user-facing language aligned with federation doctrine | Create or link a registry-owned localization key |
| Transitional | Current language still needed but scheduled for semantic replacement | Freeze new use and create migration record |
| Legacy | Historical or extinct-era language | LEGACY_IMPORTS language record; no active localization key |
| Forbidden | Language that restores abolished authority or violates dignity | Block from new runtime; require constitutional replacement |
| Non-Localizable | Route, identifier, protocol, field name, developer-only invariant | Exclude from localization inventory with reason |
| False Positive | Scanner-captured code fragment or non-human text | Remove from the qualified language queue, not from code |

### Migration Framework

1. Separate current source from `_archive`.
2. Filter non-human and false-positive candidates.
3. Determine whether the string is visible to a user.
4. Assign registry ownership.
5. Classify doctrine.
6. Link to an existing key or propose a new semantic key.
7. Define Arabic and English meaning together.
8. Migrate consumers in later implementation waves.
9. Verify context, placeholders, accessibility, and generated output.

### Priority Order

1. Safety and crisis language.
2. Authentication and account access.
3. Client-facing Personal Space and support language.
4. Provider and center registration.
5. Public discovery and marketplace language.
6. Monitoring and governance rooms.
7. Library and knowledge.
8. Developer and archived material.

## Phase 8: Executive Verdict

### Is Mental Smile Ready For A Constitutional Localization Freeze?

**NO**

### Blocking Conditions

1. There is no canonical Constitutional Vocabulary Registry with enforceable ownership.
2. Approval, rejection, request, inbox, case, session, pending, and coordination language remains in current keys.
3. One locale parity gap exists: `statusCenterFollowUp` is missing from English.
4. 106 keys have no proven non-generated consumer and require lifecycle decisions.
5. Twenty-two duplicate-meaning groups lack canonical semantic ownership.
6. The 7003 hardcoded candidates have not been qualified into user-facing, non-localizable, legacy, forbidden, and false-positive groups.
7. Generated localization files currently reflect mixed-era vocabulary.
8. Registry categories such as OWNER_ROOM, MONITORING_ROOM, POLICY_SYSTEM, and federation-level terminology are not represented by a complete governed key set.

### Required Governance Milestones Before Freeze

1. Ratify Constitutional Vocabulary V1.
2. Create the Constitutional Language Registry with one owner per term.
3. Freeze forbidden and approval-era vocabulary from new use.
4. Qualify the hardcoded candidate inventory.
5. Resolve locale parity and duplicate meanings.
6. Classify all 106 unused keys as dormant, legacy, replacement-pending, or retirement candidates.
7. Define semantic replacements for operational keys before renaming.
8. Establish terminology proposal, approval, deprecation, and migration records.
9. Add automated doctrine checks for forbidden and deprecated terms.
10. Re-audit active runtime consumers after governance migration.

## Strategic Conclusion

Mental Smile has moved architecturally toward federation, but its language still negotiates between federation and operational control.

The next milestone is not translation cleanup. It is sovereignty:

- Registries own vocabulary.
- People retain agency.
- Signals inform.
- Readiness is derived.
- Monitoring observes.
- Safety intervenes only within declared boundaries.
- The platform does not own journeys, providers, centers, or outcomes.

Only after these meanings are governed should the localization system be frozen.
