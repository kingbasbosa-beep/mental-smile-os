## Executive Summary

Knowledge is a sovereign asset inside the Mental Smile Federation because it shapes trust, operations, education, strategic decisions, institutional memory, market understanding, and future product readiness. Content should not be treated as isolated files, documents, articles, or resources. It should be treated as governed knowledge assets that can be owned, reviewed, measured, connected, archived, analyzed, updated, and retired.

Without knowledge governance, content becomes scattered, duplicated, outdated, ownerless, legally unclear, and strategically weak. A disorganized library may appear rich while actually creating confusion, risk, and operational drag.

This blueprint establishes the governance system required to transform content into a Knowledge Operating System. It defines standards for ownership, rights, lifecycle, health, priority, demand intelligence, registries, department responsibilities, knowledge flow, publication governance, and future AI assistance.

This report is governance-only. It does not create content, modify runtime, change rules, implement Firebase, create APIs, or build UI.

## Knowledge Governance Doctrine

Knowledge is an Asset.

Content is a Managed Asset.

Archive is Institutional Memory.

Strategic Intelligence is Knowledge Consumer & Analyzer.

The doctrine means:

- Knowledge must be owned.
- Content must be governed.
- Archive must preserve memory and traceability.
- Strategic Intelligence must analyze knowledge signals and recommend improvements.
- Owner decisions remain human and sovereign.

## Content Ownership Governance

Ownership types:

### Platform Owned

Created and controlled by Mental Smile.

Usage rights: broad internal and external use as approved.

Modification rights: Mental Smile may update and revise.

Publication rights: allowed according to visibility and approval status.

### Partner Owned

Created by or belonging to a partner.

Usage rights: defined by agreement.

Modification rights: limited unless explicitly granted.

Publication rights: agreement-based.

### Licensed

Used under license.

Usage rights: limited by license terms.

Modification rights: limited or prohibited depending on license.

Publication rights: only as licensed.

### External Reference

Referenced from external sources.

Usage rights: citation or reference use only unless permission exists.

Modification rights: not owned by Mental Smile.

Publication rights: must respect source rules.

### Public Domain

Public domain material.

Usage rights: broad use, subject to verification.

Modification rights: generally allowed.

Publication rights: allowed with responsible attribution when appropriate.

### Research Reference

Academic or research-based material.

Usage rights: citation, summary, and reference use.

Modification rights: not owned.

Publication rights: must preserve accuracy and citation integrity.

### Government Source

Official government or public authority material.

Usage rights: depends on jurisdiction and source policy.

Modification rights: not owned.

Publication rights: should preserve source clarity and avoid misrepresentation.

## Content Rights Governance

Each content asset should define:

- Usage Rights: how the content may be used.
- Publication Rights: where and to whom it may be published.
- Modification Rights: who may edit, adapt, or translate it.
- Retention Rights: how long it may or must be kept.
- Removal Rights: when it may be removed, withdrawn, or retired.

Rights should be clarified before content is treated as operational, public, or strategic.

## Content Health Model

Each content asset should be evaluated through:

### Freshness Score

Measures whether the content is current and reviewed on schedule.

### Quality Score

Measures clarity, accuracy, usefulness, editorial standard, and trustworthiness.

### Coverage Score

Measures how well the content covers its intended topic, category, or user need.

### Demand Score

Measures whether users, departments, support cases, search behavior, or strategic reviews show need for the content.

### Strategic Value Score

Measures connection to federation priorities, product readiness, operational support, education, or institutional learning.

### Governance Score

Measures ownership, rights clarity, review cycle, visibility status, and archive connection.

### Content Health Score

Content Health Score should be derived from the six scores above. A high quantity of content should not compensate for weak governance, stale information, unclear ownership, or low strategic value.

Recommended score meaning:

- 0: Missing or unmanaged.
- 1: Placeholder.
- 2: Foundational but incomplete.
- 3: Operationally useful.
- 4: Mature and governed.
- 5: Strategic knowledge asset.

## Content Priority Model

Priority levels:

- Low.
- Medium.
- High.
- Strategic.
- Critical.

Movement between levels should depend on:

- Demand.
- Operational dependency.
- Risk exposure.
- Strategic value.
- Publication importance.
- Content gap severity.
- Owner or department decision.

Content can be promoted when demand, dependency, or strategic value increases.

Content can be demoted when usage drops, relevance declines, risk increases, or the content becomes outdated.

## Content Registry Architecture

The knowledge system should include the following registries:

### Content Registry

Master list of all governed content assets.

### Review Registry

Tracks review dates, reviewers, review outcomes, and next review requirements.

### Ownership Registry

Tracks content owners, departments, and responsibility changes.

### Rights Registry

Tracks usage, publication, modification, retention, and removal rights.

### Publication Registry

Tracks where content is published, when it was published, and visibility status.

### Lifecycle Registry

Tracks create, review, approve, publish, monitor, update, archive, and retire stages.

These registries may remain manual until future automation is explicitly authorized.

## Department Content Responsibilities

### Archive

Produces: historical summaries and institutional memory outputs.

Reviews: archive references, memory integrity, historical traceability.

Consumes: decisions, incidents, reports, cases, policies, lessons learned.

Archives: all important institutional records.

### Strategic Intelligence

Produces: recommendations, gap insights, trend observations, knowledge analysis.

Reviews: strategic value, demand patterns, content opportunity, content risk.

Consumes: Archive records, Control Room signals, department reports, market signals.

Archives: strategic reviews, recommendations, outcomes.

### Marketing Studio

Produces: messaging, campaign content, brand-aligned communication.

Reviews: tone, consistency, audience fit.

Consumes: brand architecture, market signals, content library.

Archives: campaign learnings and messaging decisions.

### Technical Studio

Produces: technical governance notes, implementation coordination references, platform knowledge.

Reviews: technical accuracy and feasibility.

Consumes: operational requirements and future implementation plans.

Archives: technical decisions and implementation lessons when applicable.

### Education

Produces: courses, programs, learning paths, provider enablement materials.

Reviews: learning outcomes, educational clarity, content quality.

Consumes: provider needs, center needs, content library, strategic insights.

Archives: curriculum decisions, learning product evolution, review outcomes.

### Control Room

Produces: operational, support, complaint, activity, retention, and engagement signals.

Reviews: support relevance and escalation patterns.

Consumes: operational content and support guidance.

Archives: incidents, escalations, support trends, and operational signals.

### Owner Office

Produces: decisions, approvals, governance direction, activation judgment.

Reviews: strategic alignment, authority boundaries, final decision logic.

Consumes: Strategic Intelligence recommendations, Archive memory, department reports.

Archives: decisions, rationale, outcomes.

### Provider Operations

Produces: provider guidance, enablement needs, service readiness notes.

Reviews: provider-facing usefulness and operational fit.

Consumes: educational content, platform knowledge, support signals.

Archives: provider operation lessons and recurring needs.

### Center Operations

Produces: center guidance, operational readiness needs, workflow insights.

Reviews: center-facing usefulness and operational fit.

Consumes: educational content, platform knowledge, support signals.

Archives: center operation lessons and recurring needs.

## Content Intelligence Layer

The Content Intelligence Layer should include:

### Content Usage Analytics

Tracks what content is used, ignored, revisited, or relied upon.

### Content Demand Analytics

Tracks what users and departments appear to need.

### Content Decay Detection

Identifies stale, unreviewed, outdated, or ownerless content.

### Content Opportunity Detection

Identifies topics that may support growth, education, product activation, or provider/center enablement.

### Content Risk Detection

Identifies rights issues, outdated guidance, conflicting information, publication risk, and governance gaps.

## Publication Governance

Public content may include:

- Reviewed educational content.
- Provider and center enablement materials.
- Public-safe platform knowledge.
- Approved Archive publications.
- Approved Strategic Intelligence publications.

Internal-only content should include:

- Sensitive decisions.
- Raw incidents.
- Internal department performance.
- Strategic recommendations before Owner decision.
- Competitive vulnerabilities.
- Unvalidated hypotheses.

Archive-only content should include:

- Historical decisions not intended for publication.
- Retired content.
- Superseded versions.
- Legal or compliance records.
- Internal memory required for traceability.

## Final Recommendation

Mental Smile should build a Knowledge Operating System by treating every important content asset as a governed object with identity, ownership, rights, lifecycle, health, priority, demand intelligence, archive memory, and strategic value.

The recommended path is:

- Start with manual registries.
- Require Content Digital Twins for important assets.
- Assign ownership before publication.
- Clarify rights before use.
- Review content health regularly.
- Feed usage and demand signals into Archive and Strategic Intelligence.
- Use Owner decisions to guide major content evolution.
- Introduce AI assistance only after governance standards are stable.

This allows Mental Smile to grow its knowledge base for years without losing control over accuracy, trust, ownership, rights, or strategic direction.
