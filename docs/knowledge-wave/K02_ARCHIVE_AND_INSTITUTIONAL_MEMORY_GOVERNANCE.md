# K02 - ARCHIVE_AND_INSTITUTIONAL_MEMORY_GOVERNANCE

## File Path

`docs/knowledge-wave/K02_ARCHIVE_AND_INSTITUTIONAL_MEMORY_GOVERNANCE.md`

## Executive Summary

Institutional memory is a sovereign asset inside the Mental Smile Federation because it preserves the history of decisions, incidents, cases, lessons, failures, resolutions, department evolution, and strategic movement. Without memory, the federation becomes dependent on people remembering what happened, why it happened, and what was learned. That creates risk when people change, systems change, priorities shift, or operational pressure increases.

The Archive must not become a file graveyard. Storage alone does not create institutional intelligence. A useful Archive must preserve context, links, outcomes, lessons, ownership, visibility, status, and strategic relevance.

This report transforms Archive from Storage into an Institutional Memory System. It defines archive topology, digital twin standards, registries, status models, visibility governance, intelligence layers, health scoring, open loop governance, workflow, department reporting integration, and future automation direction.

This report defines governance only. It does not create code, UI, Firebase, Firestore, rules, runtime changes, automation, collections, or APIs.

## Institutional Memory Doctrine

Archive is not Storage.

Archive is Institutional Memory.

Memory is a Strategic Asset.

Past is Future Intelligence Input.

This doctrine means:

- Every important decision should remain traceable.
- Every important incident should produce resolution and learning.
- Every important case should be connected to outcome.
- Every department should preserve its evolution.
- Every lesson should be reusable.
- Strategic Intelligence should consume Archive memory to improve recommendations.

## Decision Registry

Every important decision should have a Decision Registry entry.

Required fields:

- Decision ID.
- Decision Date.
- Decision Owner.
- Decision Context.
- Alternatives Considered.
- Expected Outcome.
- Actual Outcome.
- Lessons Learned.
- Review Date.

The Decision Registry protects the federation from losing the reasoning behind decisions. It also allows future comparison between expected and actual outcomes.

## Incident Registry

Every important incident or operational problem should have an Incident Registry entry.

Required fields:

- Incident ID.
- Discovery Date.
- Severity.
- Affected Areas.
- Root Cause.
- Immediate Response.
- Final Resolution.
- Resolution Date.
- Resolution Time.
- Lessons Learned.
- Status.

The Incident Registry should not only record what went wrong. It should preserve how the federation responded and what should be improved.

## Case Registry

The Case Registry preserves important operational cases.

A case is archive-worthy when:

- It affected users, providers, centers, departments, trust, operations, or strategy.
- It required escalation or cross-department attention.
- It revealed a repeated pattern.
- It produced a lesson learned.
- It influenced a decision.
- It exposed a governance, capacity, content, or policy gap.
- It may help prevent future mistakes.

A case is not archive-worthy when:

- It is routine with no broader learning value.
- It has no strategic, operational, or historical relevance.
- It is duplicated by another archived case.
- It does not affect future decisions, quality, trust, or operations.

Routine records may be stored operationally, but not every small record needs institutional archive treatment.

## Lesson Registry

The Lesson Registry should include:

- Lessons Learned.
- Success Patterns.
- Failure Patterns.
- Unexpected Discoveries.
- Best Practices.
- Anti-Patterns.

Lessons should be connected to their source decisions, incidents, cases, departments, and outcomes. A lesson without source context becomes weak institutional memory.

## Department Historical Registry

Every department should preserve:

- History.
- Major Changes.
- Major Decisions.
- Major Failures.
- Major Successes.
- Evolution Timeline.

This prevents department knowledge from becoming trapped inside individuals or scattered across informal notes.

## Strategic Archive

Strategic Archive should preserve:

- Strategic Reviews.
- Recommendations.
- Forecasts.
- Expansion Decisions.
- Delay Decisions.
- Risk Decisions.
- Hiring and capacity decisions when strategically material.
- Product activation decisions.
- Department activation decisions.

Strategic Archive gives future Owner decisions and Strategic Intelligence recommendations a historical basis.

## Archive Status Model

Archive status values:

- Open.
- Resolved.
- Closed.
- Historical.
- Retired.
- Superseded.
- Reference Only.

### Open

The item is active and still needs action, resolution, or review.

### Resolved

The issue or case has a recorded resolution, but may still require review.

### Closed

The item is complete and no further action is expected.

### Historical

The item is preserved for institutional history.

### Retired

The item is no longer operationally active but remains preserved.

### Superseded

The item has been replaced by a newer decision, policy, lesson, or content asset.

### Reference Only

The item is preserved for reference, not active governance.

## Archive Visibility Governance

Visibility levels:

- Public.
- Internal.
- Department Only.
- Owner Only.
- Strategic Intelligence Only.
- Archive Restricted.

### Public

Safe for external publication.

### Internal

## Archive Intelligence Layer

Archive should support intelligence through:

- Pattern Detection.
- Failure Pattern Detection.
- Success Pattern Detection.
- Decision Outcome Analysis.
- Trend Analysis.
- Repeated Incident Detection.
- Knowledge Reuse Opportunities.

Archive Intelligence does not decide. It surfaces memory patterns that Strategic Intelligence can interpret and Owner can use for decisions.

## Archive Health Model

Archive health should be measured through:

### Completeness Score

Measures whether important fields, context, and outcomes are present.

### Traceability Score

Measures whether the item can be traced to source, owner, date, event, and decision context.

### Linkage Score

Measures whether related decisions, incidents, lessons, departments, and references are connected.

### Resolution Score

Measures whether open cases and incidents have response, resolution, outcome, and review.

### Strategic Value Score

Measures whether the archived item supports future decisions, strategy, risk management, or institutional learning.

### Historical Value Score

Measures whether the item preserves important history or evolution.

### Archive Health Score

Archive Health Score should be derived from the scores above. A large archive with poor traceability, weak linkage, unresolved loops, or unclear ownership should not be considered healthy.

## Open Loop Governance

Open Loop means:

Problem Recorded

↓

No Resolution Recorded

This is a governance failure when the problem is important.

Every important Incident or Case should include:

- Response.
- Resolution.
- Outcome.
- Review.

### Open Loop

An item remains open when it lacks resolution, outcome, review, or clear next action.

### Closed Loop

An item becomes closed loop when it has a documented response, final or interim resolution, outcome, lesson, and review status.

Open Loop Governance prevents the Archive from collecting problems without institutional learning.

## Archive & Strategic Intelligence Relationship

Archive preserves.

Strategic Intelligence interprets.

Owner decides.

Archive provides institutional memory. Strategic Intelligence consumes memory, detects patterns, and recommends. Owner uses memory and recommendations to make human decisions.

Automation should support memory governance, not replace human judgment. Important archive visibility, publication, resolution, and strategic decisions should remain human-governed.

## Final Recommendation

Mental Smile should build an Institutional Memory System by treating every important decision, incident, case, lesson, department change, knowledge asset, and strategic review as governed memory.

The recommended path is:

- Establish Archive Digital Twin standards.
- Maintain Decision, Incident, Case, Lesson, Department, Knowledge, and Strategic registries.
- Prevent open loops by requiring response, resolution, outcome, and review.
- Link memory items together so Archive becomes searchable institutional context, not isolated storage.
- Feed Archive memory into Strategic Intelligence.
- Use Owner decisions to close strategic loops.
- Introduce future automation only after manual governance is clear.

This prevents repeated mistakes, improves decision quality over time, preserves institutional continuity, and allows Mental Smile to learn even as people, systems, and units change.
