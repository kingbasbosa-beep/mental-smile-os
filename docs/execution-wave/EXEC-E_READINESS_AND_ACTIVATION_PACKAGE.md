# EXEC-E - READINESS_AND_ACTIVATION_PACKAGE

## File Path

`docs/execution-wave/EXEC-E_READINESS_AND_ACTIVATION_PACKAGE.md`

## Executive Summary

Readiness must be a condition for activation inside the Mental Smile Federation. Nothing should activate because it exists. Everything should activate because it is ready.

Premature activation is one of the biggest reasons operating systems collapse. A department can be named before it has governance. A product can be imagined before it has ownership. Hiring can happen before sustained need. Automation can be introduced before a process is stable. Expansion can happen before capacity exists. Each of these creates operational chaos, authority drift, unnecessary cost, and future rebuilding.

EXEC-E creates the official manual readiness and activation system for departments, products, workforce, automation, AI assistance, expansion, activation gates, activation sequence, and the first 12-month roadmap.

This package documents readiness only. It does not create Firebase, Firestore, runtime behavior, rules, UI, automation, databases, dashboards, implementation, or new execution authority.

## Readiness Framework

## Activation Doctrine

Nothing activates because it exists.

Everything activates because it is ready.

Activation should be based on evidence, governance, ownership, capacity, content, operations, health, and Owner review.

## Department Readiness Framework

Each department should be assessed through:

- Identity Readiness.
- Registry Readiness.
- Twin Readiness.
- Content Readiness.
- Reporting Readiness.
- Governance Readiness.
- Health Readiness.

Readiness outputs:

- Not Ready.
- Partially Ready.
- Operational.
- Governed.
- Federation Ready.

### Not Ready

The department is named or imagined but lacks core identity, owner, registry, twin, or governance.

### Partially Ready

The department has purpose and some documentation but lacks full operating clarity or health model.

### Operational

The department can support manual operations with defined owner, scope, reports, and basic content.

### Governed

The department has a Digital Twin, registry links, reports, health metrics, archive links, and governance boundaries.

### Federation Ready

The department can scale, coordinate across the federation, maintain health, report reliably, and preserve archive memory.

## Product Readiness Framework

Each product should be assessed through:

- Product Definition.
- Target Audience.
- Owner.
- Content.
- Operations.
- Support Model.
- Revenue Model.
- Archive Linkage.
- Risk Review.

Product readiness outputs:

- Draft.
- Pilot.
- Limited Release.
- Production.
- Federation Product.

### Draft

Product concept exists but lacks full readiness.

### Pilot

Product can be tested in limited scope with clear owner and risk review.

### Limited Release

Product can serve a controlled audience with support and operational readiness.

### Production

Product can operate consistently with content, support, revenue model, and archive linkage.

### Federation Product

Product is mature, governed, measurable, linked to health systems, and ready for scaled federation use.

## Workforce Readiness Framework

Workforce stages:

- Solo Founder.
- Assistant Support.
- Part-Time Team.
- Core Team.
- Department Team.
- Federation Workforce.

### Solo Founder

Suitable when work is strategic, early, manual, and limited.

Transition condition: recurring operational work begins delaying governance or decision-making.

### Assistant Support

Suitable when administrative or coordination burden becomes repeated.

Transition condition: specialized workstreams require dedicated skill.

### Part-Time Team

Suitable when content, technical, marketing, or operations tasks need recurring help.

Transition condition: part-time coordination becomes a bottleneck or continuity risk.

### Core Team

Suitable when multiple workstreams require stable execution.

Transition condition: departments begin forming naturally.

### Department Team

Suitable when a department has recurring workload, reporting needs, and ownership.

Transition condition: cross-department governance becomes continuous.

### Federation Workforce

Suitable when multiple departments, products, services, and intelligence cycles require coordinated teams.

## Automation Readiness Framework

Automation stages:

- Manual.
- Assisted.
- Semi-Automated.
- Governed Automation.
- Federation Automation.

### Manual

Processes are operated through documents, registries, and human review.

Transition condition: process is stable and repeated enough to justify assistance.

### Assisted

Tools or AI help with drafting, classification, summarization, or organization.

Transition condition: manual process has clear steps and review rules.

### Semi-Automated

Parts of the process are automated while human review remains required.

Transition condition: risks, exceptions, and ownership are understood.

### Governed Automation

Automation has owner, monitoring, review, fallback, audit trail, and archive linkage.

Transition condition: automation supports multi-department operations.

### Federation Automation

Automation supports scaled federation operations without replacing human authority.

## AI Readiness Framework

AI use stages:

- AI Assistant.
- AI Analysis.
- AI Recommendation.
- AI Signal Classification.
- AI Forecast Assistance.

AI may assist with drafting, classification, summarization, analysis, recommendation preparation, signal grouping, and forecasting support.

AI never decides.

AI never governs.

AI never owns authority.

AI outputs must remain reviewable, traceable, and subject to human judgment.

## Expansion Readiness Framework

Expansion areas:

- New Department.
- New Product.
- New Service.
- New Market.
- New Geography.
- New Partnership.

Each expansion review should include:

- Activation Criteria.
- Capacity Criteria.
- Governance Criteria.
- Knowledge Criteria.
- Health Criteria.

### New Department

Requires recurring work, clear mission, Digital Twin, owner, registry linkage, reporting model, and health metrics.

### New Product

Requires demand, owner, content, operations, support model, risk review, archive linkage, and activation criteria.

### New Service

Requires repeated service need, workflow clarity, delivery capacity, support model, and governance boundaries.

### New Market

Requires market evidence, brand fit, policy review, capacity review, and risk assessment.

### New Geography

Requires localization readiness, legal/policy awareness, support capacity, brand readiness, and operational model.

### New Partnership

Requires strategic fit, risk review, ownership, rights clarity, legal readiness when needed, and archive linkage.

## Strategic Trigger Alignment

The Strategic Trigger Registry from EXEC-C should be used to move signals into review.

A Trigger becomes a Recommendation when:

- evidence exists.
- threshold is clear.
- relevance is strategic.
- potential action is defined.
- confidence can be assessed.
- Strategic Intelligence can explain reasoning.

A Recommendation becomes an Activation Candidate when:

- Owner review accepts the recommendation for readiness assessment.
- readiness criteria can be evaluated.
- governance is sufficient.
- capacity, content, health, and risk are not blocking.
- archive linkage exists.

A trigger never creates automatic activation.

Scorecards:

- Product Readiness Score.

- Automation Readiness Score.
- Expansion Readiness Score.

Federation Readiness Score is derived from the scorecards above plus governance, archive, knowledge, and health readiness.

Scores guide Owner review. They do not automatically activate anything.

Passing conditions:

- Strategic Recommendation Registry exists.

### Gate 1 - Governance

Passing conditions:

- Brand governance exists.
- Asset governance exists.
- Policy alignment exists.
- Legacy governance map exists.
- governance health model exists.

### Gate 2 - Operations

Passing conditions:

- department readiness framework is applied.
- operational owner is defined.
- reports are defined.
- capacity is assessed.
- support and escalation boundaries are clear.

### Gate 3 - Knowledge

Passing conditions:

- content priority map exists.
- evidence registry exists.
- archive backlog system exists.
- strategic trigger registry exists.
- reporting calendar exists.

### Gate 4 - Scale

Passing conditions:

- workforce readiness supports expansion.
- automation readiness is assessed.
- product readiness is sufficient.
- health scores are stable.
- open loops are controlled.

### Gate 5 - Federation Expansion

Passing conditions:

- departments are governed.
- products are ready.
- archive and intelligence loops are operating.
- brand, policy, and asset governance are stable.
- Owner approves expansion.

## Activation Sequence

Logical activation sequence:

1. Owner Office.
2. Archive.
3. Strategic Planning.
4. Asset Governance.
5. Strategic Intelligence.
6. Provider Operations.
7. Center Operations.
8. Product Portfolio.
9. Marketing Studio.
10. Technical Studio.
11. Education.
12. Control Room Evolution.
13. Finance.
14. Legal.
15. Automation Layer.

Rationale:

- Owner Office and Archive come first because decisions and memory must exist before scale.
- Strategic Planning and Asset Governance come before products so expansion has control.
- Strategic Intelligence begins advisory review after evidence and archive linkage exist.
- Provider and Center Operations come before broader products because they are closest to operating value.
- Marketing, Technical, and Education activate when recurring work justifies structure.
- Finance and Legal activate when recurring complexity justifies them.
- Automation comes last, after manual workflows prove stable.

## Anti-Chaos Safeguards

Rules:

- No activation without governance.
- No hiring without sustained need.
- No automation without stable process.
- No expansion without capacity.
- No product launch without ownership.
- No department maturity claim without Digital Twin.
- No scale without registry discipline.
- No recommendation treated as decision.
- No Control Room authority drift.
- No AI authority drift.

## No Return Activation Rules

Foundational activation rules:

- Governance First.
- Archive First.
- Recommendation does not equal Decision.
- Control Room does not equal Authority.
- AI does not equal Authority.
- Twin Before Maturity.
- Registry Before Scale.

These rules cannot be bypassed without constitutional review.

## Final Recommendation

Mental Smile can grow gradually without operational chaos or constant rebuilding by making readiness the gate before activation.

The recommended operating principle is:

- Create registries before scale.
- Create Twins before maturity.
- Create governance before activation.
- Create archive memory before expansion.
- Create evidence before recommendations.
- Create recommendations before Owner decisions.
- Create stable manual workflows before automation.

This allows Mental Smile to expand in controlled stages, with each department, product, workforce change, automation layer, and market move passing through readiness review before activation.
