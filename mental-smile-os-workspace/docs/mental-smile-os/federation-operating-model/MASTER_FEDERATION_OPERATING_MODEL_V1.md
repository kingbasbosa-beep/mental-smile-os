# MASTER_FEDERATION_OPERATING_MODEL_V1

## Identity

Mental Smile OS is a federated operating system for Arabic-first mental health discovery, support, learning, commercial visibility, governance, monitoring, strategy, and archive continuity.

It is not a single dashboard, a single app menu, or one shared runtime authority. It is a federation of sovereign zones connected through gateways, signals, routes, cards, registries, and controlled runtime consumption.

## 1. What Mental Smile OS Actually Is

Mental Smile OS is the Generation 1 constitutional operating layer that defines how every active zone, screen, route, card, signal, registry, archive, and owner authority relates before runtime execution.

Runtime is the final consumer. Runtime does not invent authority. Runtime must consume the federation model.

## 2. Federation Purpose

The federation exists to:

- Separate operational zones.
- Prevent direct cross-zone mutation.
- Preserve ownership and lineage.
- Route signals through gateways.
- Keep governance separate from operation.
- Keep archive separate from runtime.
- Allow zone replacement without federation collapse.

## 3. Federation Structure

The federation is composed of:

- Zones.
- Zone Gateways.
- Signal Pipes.
- Route Systems.
- Card Systems.
- Registry Systems.
- Monitoring.
- Strategic Intelligence.
- Smart Archive.
- Owner Authority.
- Emergency Continuity.
- Runtime Lifecycle.

## 5. Gateway Network

Every zone has one primary gateway. Internal zone surfaces and districts terminate their external signals at the local gateway. Gateways collect, count, classify, summarize, buffer, forward, receive, and distribute.

Gateways may not govern, authorize, execute, mutate foreign zone data, or make strategic decisions.

## 6. Signal Network

Signals are operational events owned by their source zone. Forwarding does not transfer ownership. Signals move through gateways and approved signal pipes only.

Signals may be monitored, summarized, archived, replayed, and expired according to retention doctrine.

## 7. Route Network

Routes are constitutional movement contracts between producers and consumers. A route may be internal, gateway-bound, cross-zone, monitoring, strategic, archive, emergency, or continuity.

Routes do not create authority. Routes expose permitted movement that already exists in the federation.

## 8. Card Network

Cards describe constitutional objects, runtime assets, screens, zones, signals, routes, and packages. Cards are governance entities. Runtime consumes cards but does not become their source of truth.

## 9. Registry Network

Registries track cards, prompts, signals, routes, zones, gateways, screens, assets, ownership, archive states, and lifecycle status. No object is valid without a registry entry.

## 10. Monitoring Network

Monitoring observes gateway health, signal volume, continuity state, and escalation visibility. Monitoring may report and escalate. Monitoring may not authorize or execute.

## 11. Strategic Network

Strategic Intelligence reads summaries and reports, performs analysis, and creates recommendations. It may not mutate runtime, approve changes, or govern zones.

## 12. Archive Network

Smart Archive stores, snapshots, indexes, preserves, restores, and holds clone packages. Archive may not operate source zones or mutate active runtime.

## 13. Owner Authority Network

Owner Authority creates doctrine, approves constitutional changes, approves registry changes, approves federation evolution, and may exercise emergency authority. Owner may not bypass registries, audit, or constitutional lifecycle.

## 14. Emergency Continuity Network

Emergency continuity preserves federation survival during failures.

Required components:

- Continuity Capsules.
- Emergency Exchange.
- Zone Failure model.
- Gateway Failure model.
- Signal Replay.
- Recovery Procedures.
- Federation Survival Doctrine.

Core principle:

```text
Zone Failure != Federation Failure
```

When a zone fails, its gateway or continuity path may buffer signal packages, preserve metadata, and replay after recovery. If a zone must be replaced, the clone package and capsule replay restore continuity without requiring legacy repair.

## 15. Runtime Lifecycle

Runtime lifecycle follows:

1. Consume registered federation doctrine.
2. Load active zones only.
3. Bind gateway routes.
4. Register signal families.
5. Consume approved cards.
6. Read approved registries.
7. Activate screen and district routes.
8. Validate authority boundaries.
9. Preserve monitoring and archive visibility.
10. Reject unregistered routes, cards, assets, collections, and signals.
